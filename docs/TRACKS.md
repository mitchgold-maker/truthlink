# Tracks and judging fit

**Confirm with an organizer:** can one project compete in Track 1 (Transpose) and a Track 2 pool (Bloomerang) at the same time?

## Track 1C: Data for the Real World (Transpose Platform)
Brief: real-world work runs on guesswork; collect better data, model it precisely, and you can start to control it.

Positioning: built for large engineering organizations (aerospace and defense companies like Lockheed Martin and Boeing), proven first with student engineering club pilots.

How ProofPilot answers it:
- Replaces "I think it's done" with **verified evidence** per task.
- Builds a **measured record of capability** (demonstrated skills from verified completions, not self-reported).
- Models **capacity** (available hours, exams/PTO, load) to predict risk and pick replacements.

Show the judges: the before/after of a club project's status (guesswork vs verified), the demonstrated-skill profile updating live, and a replacement chosen by data.
Judges here value ambition and engineering quality: keep the permission and verification rules airtight.

## Bloomerang pool: selling, working and iterating with non-profits
Student engineering clubs (non-profit student organisations) are our **pilot groups**: they use the features and tell us what to change, while the paying market is large engineering companies. Evidence to collect during the event (log it in `validation/LOG.md`):
- Conversations with club leaders: the pain in their words.
- Changes made because of their feedback (iteration).
- Commitments: pilot sign-ups, "we'd use this for X", letters of intent, or payment intent from club budgets.
Never overstate: log exactly what each person said or committed to. Never imply Lockheed Martin, Boeing or any company is a customer, partner or endorser; they are the target market.
